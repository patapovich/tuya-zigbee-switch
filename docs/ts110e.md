# Tuya TS110E 2-gang dimmer (`_TZ3210_3mpwqzuu`, ZH-Z06)

A 2-gang mains dimmer module: a Tuya ZT3L Zigbee module (Telink TLSR8258) on a ZH-Z06-B controller
board, with a factory dimming MCU (HK32F030M) that is kept as it is. That MCU only listens on a
one-way UART for level frames and never answers, so this firmware does everything else on the Zigbee
module: the fades and holds (Open Dimmer's fader), the dimming curves, the wall switches S1 / S2, the
module button and the LED. Inside the module a virtual MCU (`src/zigbee/hk_dimmer.c`) stands in for an
Open Dimmer MCU, so the dimmer endpoints work as on the AVATTO ZDMS16-2 with Open Dimmer, as far as this
hardware allows. The wire protocol, the firmware's assumptions and what is still to be checked on
hardware: [ts110e_hk_protocol.md](ts110e_hk_protocol.md).

Status: in progress. It converts by OTA from the stock firmware; tested on a few units.

## What it does

The settings are those of Open Dimmer, the open-source firmware of the ZDMS16-2's dimming MCU. Their
meaning is in Open Dimmer's [FEATURES.md](https://github.com/patapovich/open-dimmer/blob/main/docs/FEATURES.md);
the [changelog](changelog_fw.md) says where the TS110E differs.

- **Per channel:**
  - `light`, min / max level, on level, power-on behaviour, dimming curve, `custom_curve`
  - fade on / off time, hold time, the slow zones of the off and of a hold
  - `brightness_fade_time`, transition times (the `transition` option, up to 600 s)
  - countdown (auto-off), start-up level, start boost, `lamp_compensation`
  - execute-if-off, `on_level_local`
- **Wall switches:** toggle, state or momentary, and `switch_mode` normal / detached / off. A momentary
  switch:
  - dims while held, learning its direction
  - goes to full on a double tap (`double_tap_full`), with `long_press_from_off` and `fast_press`
  - reports single, double, triple, hold, release and press as `action`
- **Bindings:** each wall switch has its own endpoint (3 / 4) to bind to other lights or groups, with
  `bind_mode` and double / triple press actions.
- **follow:** one channel can follow the other channel's wall switch.
- **Scenes and groups** on the dimmer endpoints (1 / 2), and `identify`.
- **Network reset:** a number of quick presses of one wall switch (`multi_press_reset_count`, 0: never).
  The module button held 3 s and let go also resets; a short press switches both channels. The module
  restarts 1 s after it has left (the lights stay as they are) and then pairs as a new device.
- **Restarts:** after a restart of the module alone (an update, a config write) the lights stay as they
  were.
- **`factory_identity`:** the stock firmware's manufacturer name, kept from the factory NV.
- **`nwk_command_security`:** whether the module's own network commands (link status, route
  discovery) go out secured; `repaired_at_start` / `repaired_at_join` mean it found them unsecured
  and repaired that (shown until the next restart). `tc_link_key_stale`: while joining was open
  to the whole network (Zigbee2MQTT's "permit join" for all, not via one device), the
  coordinator answered none of the last 3 devices that tried to join through the module, most
  likely because it no longer has the module's trust centre link key; reported at once, it ends
  with the next answer or a join of the module's own. If devices still cannot join through the
  module, re-pair it. A unit paired before this build needs a Reconfigure once: until then it
  shows no value, and the stale report does not reach Zigbee2MQTT (the configure binds the
  Basic cluster for it).

What this hardware cannot have, compared with a ZDMS16-2 with Open Dimmer:

- No update, install, health report or SWD for the dimming MCU: it keeps its factory firmware.
- `mcu_version` is the virtual MCU's (1.0.0), not the HK32's.
- No temperature of the dimming MCU.
- The output is 8-bit at the HK32 (255 levels): the lowest levels and the slow zones can look like steps.
- Whether the HK32's level is linear in the conduction time is not measured. The curves and
  `lamp_compensation` take it as linear.

## Defaults

| Setting | Default |
| --- | --- |
| `fade_on_time` / `fade_off_time` | 1.0 s / 1.5 s (an off from full takes about 1.7 s: its slow end) |
| `hold_time` | 4.0 s over min..max (from off: 0.7 s at the minimum first) |
| `fade_off_slow_zone` / `hold_slow_zone` | 15 % / 25 % of min..max |
| `min_level` / `max_level` | 1 / 254 (the HK32's levels 3 / 255) |
| `dimming_curve` | `led_soft` |
| `mcu_ramp` | 1500 (the factory ramp; only for a change made at once, and a change while that ramp runs) |
| `power_on_behavior` | `off` |
| `switch_type` / `switch_mode` | toggle / normal |
| `long_press_from_off` / `double_tap_full` | on / on |
| `start_boost`, `lamp_compensation`, `countdown` | 0 |
| `brightness_fade_time` | unset: a level change while lit takes 2 s for the whole range |
| `bind_mode` / double / triple press | auto / full / none |
| `multi_press_reset_count` | 10 (closures of one switch within 10 s of the first) |

The stock firmware's own settings (min / max brightness, switch type, power-on) are not taken over: set
them again after the conversion.

## Converting from the stock firmware

The conversion is an OTA update. The stock firmware takes the `-from_tuya` image of this firmware, and
there is no soldering. No stock image of this model is published, so there is no OTA back to stock. Only
a wired flash ([telink.md](flashing/telink.md)) of a backup made before can take a unit back.

1. **Converter first.** Put the external converter (`switch_custom.js`, generated from `device_db.yaml`)
   into Zigbee2MQTT and restart it. Its stock definition of `TS110E_2gang_2` gets OTA for
   `_TZ3210_3mpwqzuu` only, and the custom one (`TS110E-ZH-Z06`) is there before the converted module
   joins. Without it Zigbee2MQTT would take the new model for an unknown device, and Home Assistant
   would delete and re-create the device's entities.
2. **The image.** Point Zigbee2MQTT's OTA override index at an index with this board's `-from_tuya`
   entry (`zigbee2mqtt/ota/index_router.json` of a build that includes
   `MODULE_TUYA_TS110E_3MPWQZUU`, or a local one made with `helper_scripts/make_z2m_ota_index.py`).
   Then update the device from the OTA page.
   - The fork's CI builds the ZDMS16-2 only so far: build the image yourself
     ([building.md](contribute/building.md)).
   - The stock OTA client gives up when a block comes late. If the update stops, start it again: it
     resumes.
3. **The network.** The module keeps the stock firmware's network. It takes it from the factory NV at
   its first start, and comes back on its own about 15 s after the transfer.
   - If its LED blinks instead, it did not: allow joining (Permit join), and it joins as a new device.
   - Basic 0xff55 on endpoint 1 (read it in the Dev console) shows what the start found
     (`src/zigbee/stock_nv.h`). Its second byte is the result: 5 = taken now, 6 = taken earlier,
     4 = found but not taken (its security material does not check out). Its fifth byte holds the
     copies it found (bit 0 network, bit 1 keys, bit 2 link key). Bit 3 says it cleared a
     factory-reset flag the stock OTA left. Bit 4 says the stock link key with the coordinator did
     not check out and the module took the global one instead.
4. **Interview, then Reconfigure** the device in Zigbee2MQTT.
   - Restart Zigbee2MQTT first if it still shows the stock device (it can keep the stock definition until
     a restart).
   - The interview finds the wall switches' endpoints 3 / 4 and the new clusters. Configure does it
     itself when it does not see endpoint 4.
   - Reconfigure binds the reports and reads every setting once.
5. **Set the settings again** (see Defaults): `switch_type` first (a momentary wall switch is `toggle` by
   default), then min / max level, the curve and power-on.

What a conversion does not carry over:

- The groups, bindings, scenes and reporting made under the stock firmware. Zigbee2MQTT still lists the
  group memberships and binds: add and bind them again.
- Home Assistant keeps the stock definition's entities that the new one does not have. They stay
  unavailable: delete them.

## Updating a unit that runs an earlier build

Install the regenerated converter first, then the OTA, then Reconfigure. What changed for such a unit,
and the order that matters (`power_on_behavior`, the network reset by presses): see
[ts110e_hk_protocol.md](ts110e_hk_protocol.md#updating-a-unit-that-runs-an-earlier-build) and the
[changelog](changelog_fw.md).

## device_config

Leave `device_config` as it is. Its `HK` token (`HKB4C0C3C2`: S1, S2, the button, the LED) is what turns
on the virtual MCU. Without it the module sends Tuya frames to the HK32, which takes level frames only,
and the light stops working without an error. The converter refuses a config without the `HK` token.

## Endpoints

| Endpoint | Clusters | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Basic, On/Off, Level Control, Ballast, Switch Config, Scenes, Groups, Identify, Multistate Input (the gestures) | L1: bind to it, add it to a group |
| 2 | the same, without Basic | L2 |
| 3 / 4 | On/Off, Level Control and Scenes clients | the wall switches S1 / S2: Bind source endpoints, for lights or groups to follow them |
